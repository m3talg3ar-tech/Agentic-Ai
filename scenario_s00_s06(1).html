S-04  ·  Decision 1 of 3  ·  Transparency
"Danielle was never told that an AI — not a human — decided her fate. Should Arbor Capital now require ArcHire to disclose this to all future candidates?"
Option A  ·  Governance-Aware
Yes — require disclosure to all future candidates
Mandate that ArcHire notify every applicant that an AI system will make the initial screening decision before their application is assessed.
Option B  ·  Business as Usual
No — disclosure isn't legally required right now
There is no current federal law mandating AI disclosure in hiring. Requiring it proactively could create unnecessary applicant anxiety and competitive disadvantage.
✓  Governance Principle: Transparency
Strong choice. Candidates have a fundamental right to know when a consequential decision is being made by a machine rather than a human. Without disclosure, they cannot question, contest, or understand the rejection. This is the foundation of informed consent in AI-mediated processes.
📋  Policy Connection: EU AI Act Article 52 — transparency obligations for AI systems that make decisions about people. NYC Local Law 144 (2023) requires bias audits and disclosure for automated hiring tools.
⚠  Governance Gap Exposed: Transparency
A legally defensible position — for now. But consider: candidates subject to ArcHire have no way to know an algorithm decided their fate. They cannot appeal to a human or understand why they were rejected. Every day without disclosure, more candidates enter that same position — just like Danielle.
⚠️  Watch this space: The EU AI Act and US state laws are moving fast. Illinois, Maryland, and New York already have active AI hiring requirements. What's optional today may be mandatory next year.
