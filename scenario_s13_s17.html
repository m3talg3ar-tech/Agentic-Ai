S-13  ·  Governance Summary
Three principles.
One framework for trustworthy AI.
👁 Principle 01  ·  Transparency
Affected parties must know when AI is deciding their future
Disclosure is the foundation of informed consent. Candidates, customers, and citizens cannot contest what they cannot see.
→ EU AI Act, Art. 52  ·  OECD AI Principles
⚖️ Principle 02  ·  Accountability
Responsibility cannot be diffused across the AI supply chain
Deployers own a share of every outcome their AI system produces — regardless of who built the algorithm.
→ NIST AI RMF  ·  EU AI Act (Deployer obligations)
🔍 Principle 03  ·  Auditability
AI systems must produce records sufficient for independent review
Harm that cannot be traced cannot be remedied. Without audit capability, governance is impossible — you can't fix what you can't see.
→ NIST AI RMF  ·  NYC Local Law 144
Framework 01
EU AI Act (2024)
Binding requirements for high-risk AI systems — including hiring tools. Covers transparency, human oversight, documentation, and conformity assessments.
Framework 02
NIST AI Risk Management Framework
A voluntary but comprehensive governance playbook. Covers accountable, transparent, explainable, privacy-enhanced, and secure AI throughout the full lifecycle.
Framework 03
OECD AI Principles
International baseline: transparency, accountability, robustness, and inclusivity. The foundation many national frameworks — including the EU AI Act — are built on.
