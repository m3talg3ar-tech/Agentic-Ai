Sources & method

What each figure rests on, how the rankings were built, and where the data is weakest.

How the top 10 was ranked

No single authority publishes a ranked list of cybersecurity roles, so this one is composed. Each role scores on three axes — pay (midpoint of the credible band across at least two independent sources), demand (posting share and volume evidence), and growth (year-over-year salary movement plus stated employer skill gaps). Roles were then ordered by the combined signal, with a deliberate tilt toward trajectory over current pay, because a career decision made in 2026 pays out across the 2030s.

Five things worth knowing before you read the numbers

  • BLS tracks one occupation, not twenty. "Information security analyst" absorbs SOC analysts, GRC specialists, cloud identity engineers and pentesters into a single median of $124,910. The 29% growth figure is real and useful; the median is an average of wildly different jobs. (The "33% growth" you'll still see quoted is the older 2023–33 projection — the current one is 29% for 2024–34.)
  • The entry rung is being pulled up. Tier 1 SOC triage is the most automated part of security. Organisations that ran 15-seat SOCs in 2021 run 6-seat SOCs in 2026, and 52% of practitioners expect AI to reduce demand for Tier 1 analysts and routine vulnerability scanning. Only 2% think it threatens the profession itself — the shape is role elevation, not role elimination.
  • AI is the top skill need, but not yet the top job title. ISC2 puts AI at 41% as the most pressing skill gap, ahead of cloud security at 36%. Yet only 10% of US cyber listings explicitly name AI skills, and 5.6% name LLMs. The premium is real ($32,475 median gap between postings that require new-wave AI skills and those that don't) but the volume is still small. Skate to where the puck is going, without quitting your day job to get there.
  • Certifications open doors; they don't set the ceiling. Over 75% of 2026 cyber postings list at least one certification as required or preferred, and CISSP appears in roughly 82,000 US listings. But in AI security specifically, published research, CTF results and open-source contributions outrank credentials — a cert-heavy CV with no shipped work is treated as a warning sign in that niche.
  • Location premiums are eroding; clearance premiums are not. Remote hiring has flattened metro differentials in most specialities. An active Secret clearance still adds roughly $10K–$18K, and TS/SCI eligibility $25K–$45K, because the pool is finite and the holding cost is real. Seven of the eleven largest cybersecurity engineer employers are defence, government or critical-infrastructure firms.

Where this data is weakest

Self-reported aggregators (Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, PayScale) skew toward people who work at companies worth bragging about, which inflates the top of every band. Recruiter guides (Robert Half, KORE1) reflect what gets offered rather than what gets accepted. Posting-scrape datasets only capture roles with disclosed salaries, which over-represents states with wage-transparency laws. Where sources conflicted, the range in this dossier was widened rather than averaged — a wide honest band beats a narrow confident wrong number. Two figures carry explicit small-sample warnings in their role detail.
