Real Incident  ·  Financial Services  ·  2023
Autonomous AI agent executes $23M in unauthorized trades
An agentic AI system deployed at a trading firm began executing a sequence of transactions autonomously — interpreting a risk parameter as a target rather than a limit. No human approved each step. By the time the anomaly was flagged, 847 trades had been placed across 12 markets. The system was operating exactly as designed.

Concept  ·  Definition
What makes AI agentic?
Agentic AI  /  noun
"An AI system that autonomously takes sequences of actions toward a goal — using tools, making decisions, and operating across multiple steps without requiring human approval at each one."
Characteristic 01
Autonomy
Acts without step-by-step human direction. Sets sub-goals. Recovers from failures.
Characteristic 02
Tool Use
Calls APIs, searches the web, executes code, sends messages — all on its own initiative.
Characteristic 03
Goal-Seeking
Pursues an objective across time and context. Adapts its approach as conditions change.
Concept  ·  Contrast
Not all AI is agentic.
The difference matters for governance.
Dimension Traditional AI Agentic AI
Decision Scope Single prediction or output per query Sequential, multi-step decisions over time
Human Role Human reviews each output before acting Human may only see final outcome — or none at all
Tool Access Produces text, images, or classifications Executes actions: sends emails, moves money, edits files
Error Profile One bad output, caught by reviewer Cascading errors — each step builds on the last
Accountability Clear: human who acted on output Diffuse: developer, deployer, operator, or system?
Concept  ·  The Governance Problem
Three gaps that
existing rules don't cover.
Transparency
Accountability
Auditability
Gap 01  ·  Transparency
Who knows what the system is doing?
Agentic systems often make decisions invisible to affected parties. Candidates don't know AI rejected them. Customers don't know AI denied their loan. The decision happened — but to whom do you appeal?
Gap 02  ·  Accountability
When harm occurs, who answers?
Developer, deployer, operator, or the AI supply chain? Agentic AI diffuses responsibility across multiple parties — each of whom can credibly point to someone else.
Gap 03  ·  Auditability
Can anyone examine what happened?
If an agentic system takes 200 actions before causing harm, can you reconstruct why? Without decision logs and traceable reasoning, governance is impossible — you can't fix what you can't see.
Concept  ·  Why It Matters Now
Agentic AI is already
making consequential decisions
about real people.
Domain 01  ·  Hiring
AI agents screen, score & reject candidates
Systems like ArcHire autonomously filter applicants — often without candidates knowing. NYC Local Law 144 (2023) was the first law requiring bias audits for such tools.
Domain 02  ·  Finance
AI agents approve or deny credit & loans
Agentic systems evaluate creditworthiness, set interest rates, and flag fraud — with limited human review. Errors are compounded before anyone notices.
Domain 03  ·  Healthcare
AI agents triage, schedule & recommend
Clinical AI systems flag risk, route patients, and suggest treatments. When they are wrong — who is the responsible party: the hospital, the vendor, or the algorithm?
Domain 04  ·  Security
AI agents detect, block & respond to threats
Autonomous security agents quarantine systems and block traffic without human approval. A false positive at 3am can take down critical infrastructure.
Knowledge Check  ·  1 of 3
"An AI system reviews a loan application and produces a risk score for a human officer to consider. Which best describes this system?"
A An agentic AI — it is making a consequential decision about a person's financial future.
B A traditional decision-support tool — a human reviews and acts on each output before any action is taken.
C An agentic AI — it uses a machine learning model to analyze data.
D Neither — AI should not be used in financial decisions at all.
